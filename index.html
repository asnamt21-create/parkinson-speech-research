Research Overview

This project investigates whether speech recordings contain patterns that help distinguish Parkinson's disease (PD) recordings from healthy-control recordings. It compares handcrafted acoustic features with representations extracted from a pretrained speech model.

Primary dataset

49

IPVS B1 recordings from 46 speakers: 28 PD recordings and 21 elderly healthy-control recordings.

External dataset

37

MDVR-KCL ReadText recordings from 37 speakers: 16 PD and 21 healthy controls.

Research objective

2 models

Compare classical acoustic machine learning with a Wav2Vec 2.0 and BiLSTM classifier.

Methodology

The primary evaluation uses fixed, speaker-independent five-fold cross-validation. The separate MDVR-KCL corpus is used for external cross-corpus evaluation.

Deep-learning pipeline

1. Audio recordings 2. Mono, 16 kHz preprocessing 3. Five-second chunks 4. Frozen Wav2Vec 2.0 5. 768-dimensional embeddings 6. BiLSTM classifier 7. PD / healthy-control classification

Classical baseline

171 acoustic predictors, including MFCC, delta and delta-delta summaries, fundamental frequency, jitter and shimmer. Multiple classical classifiers and feature strategies were evaluated.

Deep-learning model

Frozen facebook/wav2vec2-base embeddings are mean-pooled per audio chunk and passed to a one-layer bidirectional LSTM classifier.

Interpretation: Both approaches show high internal performance on IPVS but substantially weaker performance on the external corpus. This highlights a cross-corpus generalisation challenge; the study cannot isolate the effects of language, recording conditions, task or speaker population.

Limitations and Responsible Use

The primary cohort is small, and performance on one corpus does not guarantee performance on other populations or recording environments. External balanced accuracy and ROC-AUC do not provide clear evidence of discrimination beyond the conventional 0.50 reference in this sample.

These models are research classifiers, not validated clinical diagnostic tools. Results must not be used to diagnose Parkinson's disease or replace assessment by qualified healthcare professionals.

Project Summary

This study demonstrates an end-to-end speech classification workflow covering audio preprocessing, acoustic feature extraction, pretrained speech representations, speaker-independent model evaluation, external validation and uncertainty analysis.

This demonstration presents MSc research findings and an interactive speech analysis interface for academic evaluation. Predictions are strictly non-clinical and must not be used for diagnostic purposes.
